# The Gloom

## How to read a Gloom

Six ready to run. Each one is a real neglect first and a monster second, and
that order matters — if you can only remember one thing from this chapter,
remember that you build these out of paperwork rather than out of teeth.

Every entry has five parts:

- **Clock** — how many segments, all of them full at the start.
- **In the everyday** — the ordinary, unglamorous thing this actually is.
- **In the Spectrum** — the body it has when the light splits.
- **Moves** — the three things it does on a Miss. Not attacks; things it *does*.
- **When it falls** — the one concrete thing that changes back home.

None of them has a personality, a plan, or a line of dialogue. They are not
being cruel. They are shapes that things fall into.

---

## The Last Bus — clock 6

**In the everyday:** A night bus route, cancelled. The 41, which used to run
until half twelve and now stops at nine. Nobody protested; there was a
consultation and eleven people responded.

**In the Spectrum:** A bus the length of a street. Three storeys of chrome and
rust, windows lit from inside by something warm that you cannot quite see into.
Headlights like dead suns. It is **always pulling away** — never arriving,
never chasing. The doors are always closing, and there is always one more
person on the pavement.

**Moves:**
- *Strand someone.* A Star finds themselves alone at a stop that was crowded.
- *Make the distance longer.* Where they are going is further than it was.
- *Convince them it was always like this.* Was there a 41? Are they sure?

**When it falls:** The route runs again on Fridays. Two people who had stopped
seeing each other are on it, and neither planned that.

---

## The Quiet Shelf — clock 4

**In the everyday:** A library open two days a week instead of five. The
reading group folded. The photocopier has a sign on it.

**In the Spectrum:** Shelves that grow taller and further apart the longer you
walk between them, until the aisles are canyons and the ceiling is a rumour.
Warm, silent, and entirely indifferent. A returns slot high in one wall that is
always about a foot out of reach.

**Moves:**
- *Shorten the hours.* A door that was open is closed, with a laminated note.
- *Lose the thing somebody needed.* It was here. It is catalogued. It is gone.
- *Empty a room.* The people who were here are not, and were not, ever.

**When it falls:** The Tuesday reading group starts again. Four people. One of
them is new.

---

## The Silt — clock 6

**In the everyday:** A stretch of river nobody clears. Two shopping trolleys, a
bike frame, and the slow accumulation of everything that is technically
somebody else's job.

**In the Spectrum:** A chrome tide, mirror-bright and thickening, moving at the
speed of a minute hand. It swallows landmarks one at a time and reflects them
back very slightly wrong — the footbridge with one railing too many, the church
with its clock at a time it has never been.

**Moves:**
- *Thicken.* Movement costs more than it did.
- *Swallow a landmark.* Something the Stars were navigating by is under it now.
- *Make it somebody else's job.* Whoever should deal with this, it is not them.

**When it falls:** You can see the bottom by the footbridge again. Somebody
takes a photograph of it, which is a strange thing to photograph.

---

## The Long Weekend — clock 4

**In the everyday:** Somebody nobody has rung in a month. Everyone assumed
someone else had. Everyone was wrong in the same direction.

**In the Spectrum:** A very comfortable room that keeps adding doors, all of
which lead back into it. Deep chairs. Good light. A blanket that is exactly
warm enough. A voice — not threatening, genuinely kind — that keeps agreeing
that you are probably too tired for that tonight.

**Moves:**
- *Make it easier to stay in.* The chair is very good. The rain has started.
- *Cancel a plan.* Somebody texts to say no worries, another time.
- *Offer a good excuse.* And it is a good one. That is the trouble.

**When it falls:** They answer the phone. They are embarrassed about how long it
has been. They come out anyway.

**A note:** This one is the closest to somebody's real life at almost every
table. Ask first.

---

## The Grey Ledger — clock 8

**In the everyday:** A community centre being wound down by committee. Nobody
decided to close it. It is being reviewed, and then reviewed again, and the
boiler is not being fixed in the meantime.

**In the Spectrum:** An enormous filing structure — shelves and drawers and
pneumatic tubes going up past where the sky should be — that **reclassifies
whatever it touches.** A football pitch becomes an asset. A choir becomes a
line item. A person becomes a case. Everything it processes comes out true,
accurate, and completely drained.

**Moves:**
- *Reclassify.* Something the Stars care about is now a category.
- *Require a form.* There is a process for this. The process is not available.
- *Promise a review.* In due course. Nobody is saying no.

**When it falls:** The booking system reopens. Somebody books the hall for a
birthday party, on a Saturday, for forty people.

---

## The Understudy — clock 8

**In the everyday:** Burnout. Specifically the belief — arrived at quietly, over
months — that your effort was never the point and the thing would have happened
anyway.

**In the Spectrum:** A figure that is almost exactly a Star. Same silhouette,
same colour, slightly better. It does their Signature more cleanly than they
do. It is not mocking them; it is genuinely, patiently helpful, and it keeps
offering to take over so they can rest.

**Moves:**
- *Offer to do it for you.* And it would do it well.
- *Be better at being you.* It performs a Star's own Signature, correctly.
- *Ask why you bother.* Not cruelly. As though it would like to know.

**When it falls:** The Star it was shadowing does one small ordinary thing
badly, on purpose, in front of everyone, and it is fine.

**A note for the Showrunner:** This is the only Gloom that is about the Stars
themselves rather than the town, and it needs a lighter hand than its clock
size suggests. Never let The Understudy be contemptuous — the moment it sneers,
it becomes a villain and stops being frightening. It should be *kind*. Check in
before you run it, and again after.

## Building your own

Chapter 7 has the five-step procedure. Two additions now that you have seen six
of them.

**Give it one verb.** Every Gloom above is doing exactly one thing forever.
Pulling away. Growing taller. Thickening. Getting more comfortable.
Reclassifying. Offering. If you cannot say your Gloom's verb in one word, it is
not built yet.

**Write the "when it falls" line first.** Before the body, before the moves,
before the clock. That sentence is the reason the table is spending an evening
on this, and if it does not make *you* feel something in prep, it will not
survive contact with the Big Finish.

Then check it against three questions:

1. Could a Star point at the everyday version?
2. Is it doing something rather than wanting something?
3. Is the thing that comes back small, concrete, and specific?

Three yeses and you have a Number.

## Reskinning these six

You will run out of ready-made Glooms faster than you expect, and the fastest
way to make more is to keep the *skeleton* and change the subject.

Each of the six above is really a shape:

| Gloom | The shape underneath |
|---|---|
| The Last Bus | A connection that used to exist and now does not |
| The Quiet Shelf | A place that was free and is now conditional |
| The Silt | A slow accumulation nobody owns |
| The Long Weekend | A kindness that has become an excuse |
| The Grey Ledger | A process that turns people into categories |
| The Understudy | A person who has concluded they are optional |

Any of those shapes will take a new subject without complaint. The Last Bus
becomes a phone tree that has quietly stopped being answered. The Quiet Shelf
becomes a park that now needs booking. The Silt becomes a group chat that
nobody has read in five months, thickening with everything unsaid.

Keep the clock size, keep the three moves, rewrite the body and the "when it
falls" line. Twenty minutes of prep, and it will not feel like a repeat,
because the everyday problem is what your players actually engage with and that
part is entirely new.

## What Glooms never do

A short list, because the temptation is real and it will ruin them.

**They never taunt.** No dialogue, no gloating, no monologue. The moment a
Gloom explains itself it becomes a villain, and villains can be argued with,
and a thing you can argue with is not frightening in this particular way.

**They never target a Star personally** — with one deliberate exception, The
Understudy, which is why that one needs care. Glooms do not have grudges. They
have appetites, and appetites are not about you.

**They never adapt.** They do not learn from the Stars' tactics, counter their
Signatures, or change plan between rounds. They cannot. Whatever they were
doing in the Verse, they are still doing in the Big Finish, only closer.

**They never win by escalating.** The clock does not grow. The only way a Gloom
takes the night is by grinding five people down until every one of them is
Dimmed, and that takes a long, bad run of dice and a group who stopped helping
each other.
