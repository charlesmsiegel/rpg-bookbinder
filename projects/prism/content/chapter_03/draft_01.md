# Making a Star

## The twenty-minute procedure

You make Stars together, out loud, in one sitting. Not on your own at home the
week before — together. Half of what you are building is the web between you,
and you cannot build that alone.

Work through these in order. Do not skip ahead; each step feeds the next.

1. **Traits** — what you are good at. Five minutes for the table.
2. **Refrain** — your one line. Say it out loud when you have it.
3. **Signature** — the thing you are unmistakably about.
4. **Look** — who you are before any of this starts.
5. **Radiance** — who you are when you are most yourself. Take your time here.
6. **Bonds** — one line to each other Star. This is the long bit.
7. **The team** — a name, and the word you all shout.

Twenty minutes if you keep moving. Longer is fine. If you would rather play
tonight and build later, Chapter 6 has five finished Stars waiting.

## Traits: Heart, Flash, Craft, Cool

Four Traits. Assign them: one **+2**, two **+1**, one **+0**.

That spread is fixed. Everyone at the table has the same shape — one thing they
are great at, two they are fine at, one they are not. What differs between
Stars is *which*, and everything else on the sheet.

- **Heart** — sincerity, courage, connection, standing your ground, meaning it.
  Heart is not kindness. Heart is the willingness to be seen wanting something.
- **Flash** — speed, grace, performance, physical daring. Doing the difficult
  thing *and* making it look good, which in this game are the same skill.
- **Craft** — cleverness, making, fixing, knowing how a thing works. Craft is
  curiosity with its sleeves rolled up.
- **Cool** — composure, style, reading a room, being the one who does not
  panic. Cool is not detachment. Cool is holding steady so others can wobble.

Pick the +2 first — it is the thing your Star is known for. Then the +0.

### The +0 is the best number on your sheet

Do not treat it as damage. It is the reason you have friends.

Here is what a +0 actually means at the table: sooner or later the thing in
front of the group will be the thing your Star is worst at. Not "difficult for
them" — genuinely outside them. And in that moment the game asks you a
question, which is the question PRISM is built to ask:

*What do you do when the problem in front of you is the one you cannot solve?*

The answer this game wants is **ask someone**. Not "try anyway and fail
forward". Not "have a different character step in". Turn to the person beside
you, out loud, in character, and ask. That is the whole design. The +0 is where
Power of Friendship stops being a nice option and becomes the only door.

So choose your +0 for drama, not for convenience. Give it to the thing your
Star most wishes they were good at. A Craft +0 who desperately wants to
understand how things work is worth ten optimised sheets.

## Your Refrain

One line. Your Star's own words. The thing they believe hard enough to lose
something over.

It is not a motto and not a catchphrase. It is a rule they hold themselves to,
phrased the way they would actually phrase it.

> *"I don't leave people in the dark."*
> *"Somebody has to be ridiculous first."*
> *"If it's broken I want to know why."*
> *"I can hold this. Someone has to."*
> *"You can tell me the bad version."*

Your Refrain is also your income. **When it costs you something in a scene,
take 1 Sparkle back** — once per scene. That is the only reliable Sparkle in
the game, so a Refrain that never costs anything will leave you broke.

Test it before you write it down: **can you imagine a scene where holding to
this line loses you something you wanted?** If not, it is a slogan. Sharpen it
until the answer is yes.

Say it out loud to the table. If it makes someone go *oh*, keep it.

## Your Signature

The one thing your Star is unmistakably about. It gets a name, and it does
something specific.

A Signature **drops a task one Difficulty band** when you are doing the thing
you are for: Dazzling becomes Tricky, Tricky becomes Easy. Or it makes
something *attemptable* that the Showrunner would otherwise not have offered a
roll for at all.

It is never a bonus to the roll. It never adds to your total. It changes what
you are up against, which is a bigger effect and a cleaner one.

> **The one who goes back.** Drops a band on any roll to reach, retrieve, or
> refuse to abandon someone.
>
> **Opening act.** Drops a band when doing something publicly, loudly, and first.
>
> **Takes it apart.** Drops a band on understanding how a thing — or a Gloom —
> actually works.

Write yours as a name plus a sentence beginning *"Drops a band when…"*. Keep
the condition narrow enough that it does not apply to everything. A Signature
that fires constantly is a broken one, and duller than a narrow one that fires
twice a night at exactly the right moment.

Your Signature also names one thing you can do **while transformed** — a
Radiance ability. Do not write it yet. Write it after Radiance, below, when you
know what you look like.

## Look

Who your Star is with the colour folded up. Before any of this starts.

Three or four specifics, no full paragraph. What they wear when nobody is
watching, how they carry themselves, one detail that is theirs.

> *Cargo coat two sizes too big, permanent headphones round the neck, laces
> never done.*

Keep it ordinary. The Look is the everyday, and the everyday is not drab — it
is just unsplit. The contrast with what comes next only works if this part is
somebody you would actually walk past.

## Radiance

This is the biggest thing you will write today. Take the time.

Your **Radiance** is what you are when the light splits. Not a costume — a
truer edition. Five things:

**Colour.** Pick one and commit to it entirely. Magenta. Cyan. Sunburst yellow.
Violet. Mint. It is your colour for the rest of the game; the table will start
referring to you by it within one session.

**Silhouette.** The shape you make against the sky. A coat that trails like a
comet. Working clockwork worn as armour. A stillness the Spectrum bends around.
Skates that leave a gradient behind them. One image, held hard.

**Sound.** What people hear. A chord. A skate wheel. Brass gears settling. The
first two beats of a song only you know.

**The phrase.** What you say as you refract. It is not optional and it is not
a joke. Write it now, and say it out loud at the table, and let the moment be
slightly embarrassing — that embarrassment is the toll you pay once, and after
that it is just yours.

> *"Nobody stays behind."*
> *"Watch this bit!"*
> *"Show me the seams."*
> *"Breathe. I've got the door."*
> *"Go on. I'm still here."*

**The object,** if you have one. A wand, a compact, a bus pass, a wrench, a
pair of scissors, a photograph. Not every Star needs one. The ones who do
should say what it was *before*, in the everyday, because that is the good part.

Now go back and write your Signature's transformed ability, in one sentence,
using the imagery you just made.

## Bonds: the web

Do this last, and do it out loud, and do not rush it. It is the best twenty
minutes of the session.

Every Star writes **one line to every other Star**. Not a rating, not a label —
a specific shared truth, in your own words, about something that actually
happened between you.

Bad: *Priya is my best friend.*
Good: *Priya sat with me in the bus shelter for two hours and never once asked
what was wrong.*

Go round the table. When it is your turn, pick another Star and say your line
to that player, directly, out loud. They may add one sentence to it — a
detail, a correction, a *"that's not how I remember it"* — and then it is
written and it is true forever.

A Bond does two things:

- **It is spendable.** When you back that Star, you may spend the Bond instead
  of paying any other cost. It is the cheapest help in the game.
- **It is finite.** A spent Bond is marked and stays spent until the next
  **Encore**, where it comes back — rewritten, because something new is true now.

You cannot lean on the same friendship all night. That is the point.

And remember the rule from Chapter 2: **you cannot back someone you have never
been honest with.** Your Bonds are the record of that honesty. If two Stars
have no Bond, they cannot help each other yet, and the fix is a conversation
rather than a rules lookup.

## Naming the team, and the command word

Two last things, together, out loud.

**A name for the team.** Something they would actually call themselves. It can
be a bit rubbish. Most real ones are.

**The command word.** The thing you all shout to morph at once. Everyone
contributes; nobody vetoes. It should be slightly too much — a Synchronized
Morph is not a subtle event, and a command word that embarrasses you in the
shop is a command word that will land at the table.

> *"PRISM — SPLIT THE LIGHT!"*

Say it once now, together, so the first time is not in the middle of a scene.

You are done. Twenty minutes, and there are five people at this table who know
one true thing about each other.

## Watching one get made

Here is the procedure happening once, at speed, so you can see the joins.

> **Showrunner:** Traits first. One +2, two +1, one +0.
>
> **Player:** She's the one who fixes things. Craft +2. And she's… not brave?
> Not physically. Flash +0.
>
> **Showrunner:** So Heart and Cool at +1 each. What's her name?
>
> **Player:** Priya.
>
> **Showrunner:** Refrain. One line, her words, something she'd lose something
> over.
>
> **Player:** *"If it's broken I want to know why."*
>
> **Showrunner:** Can you imagine that costing her?
>
> **Player:** Oh, constantly. She'll stop to look at the thing while everyone
> else is running.
>
> **Showrunner:** Perfect. That's Sparkle in the bank every session. Signature?
>
> **Player:** *Takes it apart.* Drops a band when she's working out how
> something actually functions.
>
> **Showrunner:** Look?
>
> **Player:** Boiler suit with the sleeves cut off. Pockets full of things that
> aren't hers. Always slightly covered in something.
>
> **Showrunner:** And Radiance. Take your time.
>
> **Player:** Yellow. Sunburst yellow. And she's wearing… her coat is
> clockwork, actual working gears, and you can see them turning. Sound is brass
> settling. And she says — *"Show me the seams."*
>
> **Showrunner:** Say it properly.
>
> **Player:** *"Show me the seams."*
>
> **Showrunner:** Yeah. Now her transformed ability, one sentence, using that.
>
> **Player:** She can see how anything was put together, including people.
> Including Glooms.

Four minutes. Now she does Bonds with the other four, and she is finished.

Notice how much of that came out of the +0. Flash +0 made her someone who
stops rather than runs — which produced the Refrain, which produced the
Signature, which produced the image. The number you are worst at did most of
the character work.

## If you get stuck

**No Refrain is coming.** Ask what your Star would be embarrassed to be caught
caring about, then write that as an instruction to themselves.

**The Radiance feels generic.** You have probably reached for the genre instead
of the character. Go back to the Look, take one ordinary detail from it, and
make *that* the thing that goes impossible. The headphones become a corona. The
laces become light. Specific beats spectacular every time.

**Two Stars have nothing between them.** Good — that is a real answer, and it
is playable. Write the Bond as the absence: *"We have been in the same rooms
for two years and I could not tell you one true thing about him."* Then watch
what happens the first time one of them needs to back the other and finds they
cannot yet.

## Your first scene

You have a sheet. Here is how to use it without thinking hard.

**Lead with the Refrain.** In your first scene, put your Star in a position
where their line is relevant, and then hold to it in front of everyone. It
tells the table who you are faster than any description, and it usually earns
you a Sparkle inside ten minutes.

**Say the +0 out loud early.** The first time the group hits something in your
weak Trait, announce it: *"That's Flash. I'm nothing at Flash."* You are not
apologising — you are opening a door for somebody else to walk through, and
the game is built to reward them for doing it.

**Do not save your Sparkle.** New players hoard it. Three points is not a
war chest; it is roughly one morph and one reroll, and it refills every Encore.
Spend it on the first moment that feels big.

**Back somebody in the first hour.** It sounds your note, it costs you little
this early, and it establishes the habit before the Big Finish when it matters.

## The sheet, in one breath

Four Traits — one +2, two +1, one +0. A Refrain you can lose things over. A
Signature that drops a band when you are being most yourself. A Look that is
ordinary. A Radiance that is not. One Bond to every other Star, each of them a
true thing that happened.

Three Sparkle. Five Shine.

That is a complete character in a game where nothing on the sheet ever goes up,
and it is enough, because the part of your Star that grows is the part written
in other people's handwriting.

## A note for the Showrunner

You make a Star too — not one you play, but one you hold.

While the table builds, write down the one thing each player said that made
the room go quiet. The Refrain that landed, the Bond that made somebody sit
back, the phrase that got said twice because the first time was too quiet.
Those five notes are your session plan. Every one of them is a promise you can
keep in the first hour, and keeping them is most of what running this game is.

You should also be the one who insists on the parts people want to skip. Make
them say the phrases out loud. Make them do Bonds face to face rather than
writing quietly. Ask the follow-up question when a Refrain comes out vague.
Nobody at the table will push for the sincere version except you, and the
sincere version is the one they came for.
