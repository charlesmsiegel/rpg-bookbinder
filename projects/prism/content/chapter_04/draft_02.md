# Transformation

## Refracting

Crossing into the Spectrum is called **refracting**, and it is the same act as
morphing. One move, not two. You step sideways out of the everyday and you
arrive already changed, colour up, silhouette out, phrase already leaving your
mouth.

There is no ritual to learn and no gate to find. The seams are everywhere: a
bus shelter at the wrong time of night, the reflection in a shop window that
holds a half-second too long, the moment a stairwell light takes to come on.
Anywhere the everyday goes briefly uncertain about itself, you can lean on it,
and it gives.

What refracting costs and when you may do it depends on which of the three
gears you are using. There are exactly three, and this chapter is them.

## Solo Morph

You, on your own, out loud.

**Cost: 1 Sparkle** (the same Sparkle Chapter 2 tells you to spend freely; this
is the biggest thing it buys). Say your phrase. You arrive in the Spectrum transformed,
your Radiance unlocked, and you immediately **gain 2 Shine**, your track goes
to seven boxes for the duration and the two new ones arrive *filled*, not
empty.

**It ends** when the Gloom clock resolves, or at the Encore, whichever comes
first. Your Shine cap drops back to five then, and anything above it is gone.

Three limits, all of which exist because somebody would otherwise find the
loophole:

- **You can only be transformed once at a time.** Morphing while already
  transformed does nothing and costs nothing. You are already there.
- **You cannot revert early and morph again** to collect the Shine twice. Three
  Sparkle is not six Shine.
- **A Dimmed Star cannot morph.** Morphing requires you to be in the scene, and
  being Dimmed means you are not. There is exactly one way back from Dimmed and
  it is somebody coming to get you.

### Before the problem arrives

You may morph in the quiet opening of a Number, before anything has gone wrong.
Someone will want to, eventually, showing off in a kitchen, proving a point at
a bus stop.

When you do that, it is **purely descriptive**. No Sparkle. No Shine. No
Radiance abilities, no duration, and it does not count as having morphed. The
rainbow flares, everyone laughs, and it is over when the scene is.

This is not a punishment. It is so that you cannot arm yourself during the
calm and then arrive at the moment that matters unable to make the morph the
whole story is built around.

## Giving a morph airtime

When a Star morphs, **stop**.

The player narrates. Uninterrupted. Nobody rolls, nobody interjects, the
Showrunner does not describe the monster doing anything. However long they take
is how long it takes.

This is a rule, not a suggestion, and it is the rule most likely to be quietly
dropped by tables in a hurry. Do not drop it. The transformation sequence is
half of what this genre is *for*. A group that rushes through it to get to the
dice has bought the wrong game.

If a player is shy about it the first time, the Showrunner should ask two
questions and then get out of the way: *"What colour goes first?"* and *"What
do you say?"*

## Synchronized Morph

All of you, together, on the word you invented in Chapter 3.

**Cost: 1 Sparkle, paid in full by any single Star.** Not split, not shared, one person pays, and the whole team goes. Sparkle is never fractional.

That makes it cheaper for the group than everyone morphing solo, which is
deliberate. The game would like you to do this.

It unlocks every Star's Radiance, and three **shared abilities** that only
exist while the team is synchronized:

| Ability | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Formation** | Once per round, two Stars who have **both not yet acted this round** swap places in the turn order. Free, it costs position, not resources. Nobody gets a second action out of it. |
| **Chained backing** | A backer pays their cost **once**, and it covers backing two different rolls in the same round. It never puts a third die on one roll. |
| **Group finisher** | Two or more Stars each spend 1 Sparkle and act together. The Gloom removed doubles **once per participating Star**, two Stars quadruple it, three multiply it by eight. |

None of these adds a die or raises anybody's total. The numbers in Chapter 2
hold exactly as printed.

**It ends** when the Gloom clock resolves or at the Encore.

## The Chord

The Combined Form has a key, and the key is made of **one note per Star**.
Five of you, five notes; three of you, three. The gate is that *everybody*
has sounded, not that any particular number has.

**Every Star is a note.** You **sound your note** the first time in a Number
that you do any one of these:

- **Back another Star at a cost.**
- **Have your Refrain cost you something.**
- **Get pulled back from Dimmed.**

One mark each. That is all: there is no way to sound the same note twice, and
no way to sound somebody else's for them.

**The Chord is full when every note is sounding.**

Keep it visible. A row of boxes in the middle of the table, one per Star,
filled in as the night goes. Players will start watching it without being told.

### What the Chord is actually for

It looks like a resource meter. It is not. It is a question the game asks the
table, roughly two hours in:

*Has everybody had their moment yet?*

Because the Combined Form, the big one, the thing the whole genre is pointed
at, will not open until the answer is yes. Not until the quiet player at the
end of the table has backed someone, or been reached, or held to their Refrain
and lost something for it.

This will make your group turn toward whoever has been silent. That is not a
side effect. That is the single most useful thing this book does, and it does
it without ever telling anyone to be more inclusive. It just makes the
spectacular thing require everyone.

**A team cannot combine until nobody has been left out.** That is the game's
entire argument, printed as a trigger condition.

## Combined Form

Rare, enormous, and the reason the Chord exists.

**Three conditions, all required:**

1. The team has made a **Synchronized Morph**.
2. **The Chord is full.**
3. You are in the **Big Finish**.

Then the team fuses. One sheet. One entity, described together, everybody
contributes an image and the Showrunner welds them into one thing that should
not fit through a doorway.

- **Traits**: the best of each Trait across the team, **capped at +2**. A team
  who chose different specialities becomes +2 in all four, which is better than
  any of you has ever been. A team who all picked the same +2 will have a
  Combined Form with holes in it, and that is a real consequence of how you
  built your characters rather than an oversight. Spread your +2s at creation
  if you care about this, and do not if you would rather have the characters
  you want.
- **Shine**: the **sum of everyone's current Shine**. A team that arrives
  battered fuses into something fragile; a team that arrives intact fuses into
  something enormous. You are as strong as whatever you have left.
- **Duration**: until the Gloom clock resolves.
- **When it ends**: divide the remaining Shine as evenly as possible among the
  Stars. The players choose who takes any remainder. Anyone who ends on 0 is
  **Dimmed**, and needs reaching like anyone else.

## Finishers

A **Finisher** is a transformed Star spending 1 Sparkle to hit properly.

It **doubles the Gloom your roll removes**. A Hit takes 2 instead of 1. A
Flourish takes 4.

The **Combined Form's finisher empties the clock outright.** It costs 1
Sparkle, paid by any single Star, chosen by the players, and if nobody has a
Sparkle left, it is not available and you win the ordinary way, one tick at a
time, which is its own kind of good.

Name your Finishers. Out loud, at volume, before you roll. The Showrunner
should never let one go past unnamed.

## A Chord filling

Here is a whole Number's worth of Chord, compressed, so you can see the rhythm
of it.

> **Verse.** Nothing has gone wrong yet. Danny asks Marisol what happened at
> her mum's, and Marisol, whose Refrain is *"I can hold this. Someone has to"*
>, says *"it's fine"* when it is plainly not. That costs her nothing, so no
> note sounds. The Chord is empty.
>
> **Chorus.** The bus takes the first swing. Wren goes for the doors and
> misses. Toby backs Priya's next roll and pays for it by shouting her name
> across a full pavement — **Toby's note sounds.** One of five.
>
> Marisol tries to hold a door open on her own, takes a Mixed, and the
> Showrunner offers Exposure: she has to admit she cannot do it alone. Her
> Refrain cost her something. **Marisol's note sounds.** Two.
>
> **Bridge.** Someone says the command word. Everyone morphs. Priya backs
> Wren, paying a Shine — **Priya's note sounds.** Three.
>
> **Big Finish.** Wren goes down to 0 and is Dimmed. Danny spends his action
> and 1 Shine to reach her, which sounds **both their notes at once**, his for
> reaching, hers for being reached. Four and five.
>
> The Chord is full. It is round three of the Big Finish, the clock is on two,
> and somebody says *"together?"*

Read that again and notice what was *not* required: nobody had to do anything
clever, or optimise anything, or remember a rule. Five people played their
characters honestly for two hours and the Chord filled itself.

That is the intended experience. If your table has to work at filling the
Chord, something has gone wrong upstream, usually that someone has not had a
scene of their own, which is the exact problem the Chord exists to make
visible.

## What refracting looks like

Some texture for the Showrunner, because "you cross over" is not enough to run.

The crossing is not a portal and not a flash. It is more like a key change: the
same street, one semitone up, and suddenly there is more of it. The colours
that were always in the light come apart and stand next to each other. The
launderette sign is still the launderette sign, but it is nine colours now and
two of them do not have names.

Sound arrives about half a second late, so there is a beat of total silence
after the phrase and before the world catches up. Use it.

Things in the Spectrum have the confidence of things that were always there.
The dolphins over the ring road do not react to you. The glitter that settles
on your shoulders is warm. Nothing over there is trying to convince you it is
real, which is precisely why it is unnerving the first time.

And it is *the same place*. Whatever the Star knows about the everyday version
still applies. The bus route still runs the way it runs. The library still has
a broken window at the back. That continuity is what makes winning matter: you
are not visiting a fantasy world, you are seeing your own one with the lid off.

## Which gear, and when

Three gears, one question each.

**Solo Morph** — *is this my moment?* Use it when the scene belongs to your
Star: when the thing in front of the group is yours to answer, or when you are
the only one still standing, or when you have just said something true and want
the world to change colour around you for saying it. It costs the most per
person and it buys the most attention.

**Synchronized Morph** — *is this our moment?* Cheaper for the group, and it
opens the shared abilities, so mechanically it is usually correct. But use it
when the fiction has earned a shout, not merely when it is efficient. A
Synchronized Morph is five people agreeing out loud at the same time; spend it
where that means something.

**Combined Form**: you do not choose this one. It arrives when the Chord is
full and the Big Finish is running and somebody looks up and says *"together?"*
If you find yourself planning it in advance, you are playing the wrong game
slightly. Let it be the thing that happens *to* you.

A useful default for a first Number: nobody morphs in the Verse, one or two
Solo Morphs when the Chorus goes badly, and the Synchronized Morph at the
Bridge as the answer to whatever the Chorus did to you.

## One last thing about the phrase

Say it every time.

Not a shortened version, not "I do my morph thing", the actual words, at
actual volume, every single time your Star refracts. It will feel silly in
session one and it will stop feeling silly somewhere in session two, and by
session three somebody at your table will get a genuine shiver when they hear
it, and that is the entire reason this game exists.
