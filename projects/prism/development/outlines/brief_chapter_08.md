# Brief — Chapter 8: Running the Game

**Target**: 3300 words (±25%: 2475–4125)
**Outline**: `development/outlines/supplement_outline.md`

## Goals
Equip a first-time Showrunner to run a whole Number tonight.

## Key content
- What the Showrunner does, and the two things they never do (kill a Star,
  refuse a rescue).
- Building a Number: pick a real problem, size the clock, find the Bridge.
- The four beats in practice, with what to do when each runs long or short.
- **Making the Bridge land on time** — techniques, and what to do when a player
  morphs early (it becomes the Bridge).
- Showrunner moves, as a list, with the rule that a Miss's clock tick *is* the
  move.
- Tone dials: how bright, how sincere, how much everyday.
- Common table problems: the quiet player (the Chord already helps), the table
  that won't be sincere, a Gloom that resolves too fast.

## Design principles
- Address the Showrunner directly, in second person.
- The chapter should make running PRISM sound easy, because it is.

## Non-negotiables
- No Trait above **+2**. Difficulties are **6 / 9 / 11**.
- `morph` is the verb; `Transformation` the noun; `transformed` the adjective.
  Never "transform" as a verb.
- **Showrunner**, not Gamemaster. **Star**, not player character.
- Every probability comes from `calculate_sum_probability` or
  `references/prism/02-probability.md`. Never estimate.
- Cross-references use anchors from `heading_id_registry.md` only.
- Nothing gates transformation, role, or register on gender; the text never
  remarks on this.
