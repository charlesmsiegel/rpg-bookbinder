# Brief — Chapter 1: Welcome to the Show

**Target**: 1800 words (±25%: 1350–2250)
**Outline**: `development/outlines/supplement_outline.md`

## Goals
Sell the game in 300 words, then make a total newcomer feel safe and equipped.

## Key content
- What you do: you and your friends cross into the Spectrum, morph, and beat
  the dullness eating something you love.
- The two layers, briefly. Chapter 7 does the real work.
- What you need: 2d6 per person (or one shared pair), paper, 3-4 friends, an
  evening.
- **Safety and consent, before any rule** (500 words). Lines and veils, an
  X-card or equivalent, checking in, and the explicit note that a game this
  sincere needs *more* care about consent, not less, because players are being
  asked to be earnest in front of each other.
- How to read the book: which chapters a Showrunner needs, which a player needs.
- "A note on who gets to shine": transformation is for every character.
  State it once, plainly, and never belabour it again.

## Design principles
- The pitch must contain a concrete image (robot unicorns, a bus the length of
  a street) inside the first paragraph.
- **This chapter holds the one licensed use of "player character"** — once,
  defining the term, immediately before renaming them Stars.

## Non-negotiables
- No Trait above **+2**. Difficulties are **6 / 9 / 11**.
- `morph` is the verb; `Transformation` the noun; `transformed` the adjective.
  Never "transform" as a verb.
- **Showrunner**, not Gamemaster. **Star**, not player character.
- Every probability comes from `calculate_sum_probability` or
  `references/prism/02-probability.md`. Never estimate.
- Cross-references use anchors from `heading_id_registry.md` only.
- Nothing gates transformation, role, or register on gender; the text never
  remarks on this.
