# Themes

Four. Each one has a mechanic, or it isn't a theme, it's a mood.

## 1. You get stronger by being known

**Statement:** Power in PRISM comes from other people choosing you.

**Mechanically:** Power of Friendship. A backer adds a die and you keep the
best two; backing always costs the backer. The Chord gates the Combined Form
behind every Star having had a moment. You cannot back someone you have never
been honest with.

**Narratively:** Bonds are written as specific shared truths, not relationship
scores. They are spent, and they come back changed.

## 2. Being ordinary is not a deficiency

**Statement:** The thing you are worst at is the interesting part of you.

**Mechanically:** The fixed 2/1/1/0 spread. No advancement, no point-buy. The
+0 is where Power of Friendship stops being optional.

**Narratively:** Chapter 3 teaches the +0 as a creative prompt — *what do you
do when the thing in front of you is the thing you're worst at?* The answer
the game wants is "ask someone."

## 3. Neglect is the enemy, not evil

**Statement:** The Gloom doesn't want anything. That's the horror of it.

**Mechanically:** Gloom clocks start full and endure rather than grow — a Miss
adds a tick back but never past the starting size. The Gloom wins only by
grinding the team down, never by escalating.

**Narratively:** Every Gloom is a real local neglect with a Spectrum body: a
cancelled bus route, a library losing its funding, a neighbour nobody checks
on. Winning changes the everyday thing.

## 4. Transformation is self-expression

**Statement:** Becoming more yourself, loudly, in front of people.

**Mechanically:** Radiance is the sheet's largest creative prompt. Three morph
gears, all open to every character, none gendered. A morph gets uninterrupted
airtime and nobody rolls during it.

**Narratively:** The magical-girl register (sincere, personal, ceremonial)
fused with the sentai one (collective, commanded, colour-coded). No character
is ever told a look, a power, or a register is not for them, and the book
never remarks on this — it simply reads that way.
