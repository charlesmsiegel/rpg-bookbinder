# Setting

## The rule

**The setting is the reader's own town.** PRISM ships a procedure, not a
gazetteer. The four worked examples in Chapter 7 are models to imitate and
throw away.

## The two layers

**The everyday** — the modern world, exactly itself. No masquerade, no secret
academy, no chosen bloodline. Just the ordinary business of things being
allowed to go grey: the library losing its funding, the river nobody cleans,
the bus route they cancelled, the neighbour nobody checks on.

**The Spectrum** — the same places in howling airbrushed rainbow. Chrome and
neon and leopard print, impossible animals, gradient skies. What is a funding
shortfall in the everyday is, over there, a monster made of the exact shape of
that neglect, and it can be found, named, and hit.

Both are real. Never call the everyday "the real world."

## Refracting

Crossing over. It is the same act as morphing — you refract and arrive
transformed, one move, not two. It happens at the Bridge.

## The conversion procedure (Chapter 7's payload)

1. Name a real local problem. Small and specific beats large and abstract.
2. Ask what it costs, and who it costs. That is the Gloom's diet.
3. Build the Spectrum body: the shape that neglect takes when it has one.
4. Size the clock: 4 for a bad afternoon, 6 for a real problem, 8 for a
   season's antagonist.
5. Decide what comes back when it falls. Be concrete. The bus runs again.
